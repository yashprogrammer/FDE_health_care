1. The problem

Why insured patients wait so long to go home.

In simple words When a doctor says a patient can go home, someone has to write a discharge summary (a report of the whole stay). For insured patients, the insurance company must also approve the bill, and it needs that summary. Writing it is slow, and it often misses things the insurer wants, so the insurer sends it back.

Our solution in one line

The moment the doctor says "go home", AI writes a first draft of the summary, a checklist flags anything the insurer will ask for, and the doctor reviews and signs it, all inside the hospital's existing system.

2. The old system (MediTrack)

The hospital software we must work with, not replace.

In simple words MediTrack was built in 2011. The company that made it no longer exists, so nobody can change its code. Patient information is spread across many screens. But it has three "doors" we can use safely without touching its code.

3. The POC: high-level design

A small test app to prove the AI is safe and useful before touching the real system.

In simple words We took 11 old patient records, removed names and IDs, and built a simple app in Streamlit. It lets us generate AI drafts, check them for mistakes, score them against what the doctors actually wrote, and collect doctors' opinions. It is not connected to the hospital system.

The most important design rule

Part of the summaryWho writes it
Diagnosis, medicines, pending reportsCopied straight from hospital records. Never written by AI, because a mistake here could harm a patient.
Insurance checklistSimple rules in code. We already know what insurers ask for.
The story of the stay: symptoms, what happened each day, test highlights, condition at discharge, follow-upAI writes the draft. Turning messy notes into a clear story is what AI is good at. The doctor can edit everything.

Tools used

Streamlit (screens) · Pydantic AI (talks to the AI and makes it return a fixed format) · Groq gpt-oss-20b (the AI model) · Logfire (records every AI call so we can inspect it)

4. The POC: how it works inside

What happens when you click "Generate".

The two safety checks

CheckWhat it does
Fact checkEvery medicine, test name and number in the AI text is looked up in the patient's record. Green = found. Yellow = number not found. Red = not in the record at all (the AI made it up).
Insurance checklist7 simple rules, e.g. "is there a final diagnosis?", "is the stent sticker attached?", "are pending reports mentioned?". Gives a readiness score such as 71%.

If the AI is not available

The app never shows an empty screen. It tries, in order: live AI → a saved copy of an earlier AI draft → a basic draft built directly from the notes.

Results on the 11 test patients

<1 secondper draft, about ₹0.02 each

What we learned: the AI once wrote wrong dates, and the fact check caught them. Follow-up advice is often missing because doctors don't write it in their notes. That's a process fix, not an AI fix.

5. The integrated system: high-level design

The real thing, running next to MediTrack.

In simple words The Copilot runs beside MediTrack and connects only through the three safe doors. It reads from the reporting copy, shows the draft in a new tab inside MediTrack, and sends back the signed summary through the scanner folder. MediTrack's code is never changed, and doctors never leave MediTrack.

Why a tab, not a new app

300+ doctors and nurses already know MediTrack. A new app means new logins, training and resistance. A tab that looks exactly like MediTrack (same grey style, same buttons) means the only new habit is: "after Discharge Advised, open the AI tab."

Built-in safety

  • MediTrack is never written to directly: the Copilot only reads the copy and uses the scanner folder.
  • A doctor must sign: nothing reaches the patient or insurer without it.
  • Personal details are removed before anything is sent to the AI.
  • Every step is logged, including who edited and who signed.
